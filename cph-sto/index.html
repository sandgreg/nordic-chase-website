The route

Copenhagen → Stockholm, chapter by chapter.

Chapter 1

North Zealand & the Øresund

km 0–63

Road: past Fredensborg Palace (km 40) to Kronborg in Helsingør. Gravel: through Jægersborg Dyrehave, past Frederiksborg Castle (km 46), Gribskov forest and Esrum Lake, out to Hornbæk, then Kronborg (km 82). Both: twenty minutes across the Øresund to Helsingborg.

    Chapter 2

    Skåne & Söderåsen

    km 63–175

    Bjuv, Kågeröd and Ljungbyhed at the foot of the Söderåsen ridge, a national park of deep ravines and beech forest. Perstorp, then Hässleholm (road) or Bjärnum and Hästveda (gravel), to Osby.

      Chapter 3

      IKEA country & the lakes

      km 175–300

      Älmhult (road km 204), where IKEA began, Lönsboda and Vislanda, to the lakeside cathedral city of Växjö (km 299 / 314).

        Chapter 4

        The Småland highlands

        km 300–450

        Rottne, Braås, Åseda, Virserum, Mariannelund and Hultsfred. Forest, lakes and red farmhouses, and the roof of the route at around 280–295 m (km 355 road, km 375 gravel).

          Chapter 5

          Vimmerby to the Baltic

          km 450–600

          Vimmerby (km 457 / 455), Astrid Lindgren's hometown, then Södra Vi, Åtvidaberg and a long forest stretch to Söderköping and Mem (km 594 / 591), where the Göta Canal meets the Baltic.

            Chapter 6

            Bråviken & the Sörmland coast

            km 600–720

            Across the Vikbolandet peninsula to the free Skenäs ferry over the Bråviken inlet (km 621 / 617), Stigtomta, then Nyköping and its castle (km 655 / 651), Oxelösund, Trosa (road), known as "the end of the world", and Tullgarn royal palace nearby.

              Chapter 7

              Into Stockholm

              km 720–784

              Across the island of Mörkö and the three-minute Skanssund ferry to Hörningsnäs (km 734 / 728), then Pershagen, Tullinge and Huddinge, through Årsta and over the water into central Stockholm.

                Sleeping on the road

                You choose where and when you sleep. Some riders book hotels along the way, others carry a bivvy and stop wherever the night finds them. The rules differ per country:

                CountryWild camping
                NetherlandsNot allowed
                GermanyNot allowed
                DenmarkOnly in designated free-camping forests. Free shelters across the country (find them on udinaturen.dk)
                SwedenAllowed for a night under the right of public access (allemansrätten), away from houses and farmland
                NorwayAllowed for a night under allemannsretten, at least 150 m from houses

                You may book accommodation in advance once the rider manual is shared and published. Before that the route can still change.

                Good to know
                • Three ferries, all free for Nordic Chase riders: Helsingør–Helsingborg (every 15–30 minutes, day and night, free through our ferry partnership), then two short Swedish road ferries: Skenäsleden across Bråviken (km 621 road, km 617 gravel) and Skanssundsleden from Mörkö to Hörningsnäs (km 734 road, km 728 gravel). The two Swedish ferries usually run from around 05:30 in the morning until the evening, not through the night. Timetables can change, so check the latest times for Skenäsleden and Skanssundsleden before you plan your night.
                • Sweden's right of public access means you can camp almost anywhere for a night (see Sleeping on the road).
                • Between Växjö and Söderköping towns are small and far apart: refill when you can.
